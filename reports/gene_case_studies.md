## TP53 | A damage-responsive transcription factor

p53 can activate checkpoint genes such as `CDKN1A` (p21) and mediate growth arrest or apoptosis, depending on the cellular context. In CellAge's cited study, Sugrue et al. withdrew tetracycline to induce wild-type p53 expression in EJ human bladder carcinoma cells lacking functional p53. They observed durable arrest at G1 and G2/M, measured DNA synthesis and cell-cycle distribution by BrdU incorporation and DNA-content flow cytometry, and assessed recovery by colony formation after switching the transgene off. The cells enlarged and flattened, stained positive for SA-β-gal and accumulated lipofuscin-like inclusions. The experiment demonstrates that p53 restoration was sufficient for rapid senescence in this tumor model. The three CellAge labels do not establish three different triggers. [Sugrue et al., 1997](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC23243/)

**GO evidence.** The pinned accession P04637 has `GO:0090398` *cellular senescence* (IMP, PMID 12080348) and `GO:0090399` *replicative senescence* and `GO:0090403` *oxidative stress-induced premature senescence* (both IMP, PMID 19951988). `GO:2000774` *positive regulation of cellular senescence* carries IDA (PMID 29474172), ISS (GO_REF:0000024) and IEA (GO_REF:0000107). The damage/checkpoint rows include `GO:0006974` *DNA damage response* (IDA and IMP, five references), `GO:0030330` *DNA damage response, signal transduction by p53 class mediator* (IDA and IMP), and `GO:0031571` *mitotic G1 DNA damage checkpoint signaling* (IMP, PMID 7958916). For `GO:0051726` *regulation of cell cycle*, the matrix retains IDA, IMP, IGI and ISS separately. The IDA entry for `GO:0032211` *negative regulation of telomere maintenance via telomerase* cites PMID 10597287. Li et al. measured inhibition of telomerase activity by recombinant p53 in human breast cancer nuclear extracts. The assay did not measure long-term telomere maintenance or senescence in intact cells. [Phase 4 annotations](go_gene_annotations.csv), [Li et al., 1999](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/10597287/)

**Independent evidence and limits.** Binet et al. depleted p53 in pre-senescent human MRC5 fibroblasts. Those cells proliferated beyond the control replicative arrest, which the researchers assessed with proliferation and SA-β-gal. That loss-of-function experiment supplies replicative evidence in a distinct model and does not retroactively verify CellAge record 870. In the DNA-damage literature, irradiated normal human fibroblasts showed prolonged p53-dependent G1 arrest, and Herbig et al. traced a telomere-associated ATM–p53–p21 checkpoint in cultured human fibroblasts. These experiments relate damage sensing to cell proliferation limits, while the EJ study tests restoration in a tumor line. [Binet et al., 2009](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7439003/), [Di Leonardo et al., 1994](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/7958916/), [Herbig et al., 2004](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/15149599/)

The pinned `GO:0008340` *determination of adult lifespan* rows use mouse-based ISS and IEA (GO_REF:0000024 and GO_REF:0000107, both with mouse UniProtKB:P02340). The primary paper behind the IDA *positive regulation of cellular senescence* row reports a separate whole-animal result. Reducing `Trp53` dosage increased survival in severely short-lived `Sirt6`-null mice. The investigators also found fewer senescence-associated phenotypes in embryonic fibroblasts from the compound mutants. The survival result applies to this disease model and genetic background. The human GO target and the animal experiment describe different levels of evidence. [Phase 4 annotations](go_gene_annotations.csv), [Ghosh et al., 2018](https://elifesciences.org/articles/32127)

## CDKN2A | Two proteins at the same locus

The locus encodes p16^INK4A (P42771), which inhibits CDK4/6 and limits RB phosphorylation, and p14^ARF (Q8N726), which regulates p53 through MDM2. The CellAge citation concerns p16. Kato et al. added recombinant wild-type antennapedia–p16 fusion protein to early-passage normal human TIG-3 diploid fibroblasts. The antennapedia tag enabled protein entry. Across assays, comparators included buffer, p16 without the entry tag and a functionally impaired tagged p16 variant. The authors measured decreased tritiated thymidine incorporation and RB phosphorylation, G1 arrest and SA-β-gal staining in enlarged cells. Arrest and staining persisted seven days after removing the protein. The treated cells acquired features of replicative senescence early in passage. The paper provides direct protein perturbation evidence and does not test endogenous CDKN2A activation or a separate effect of p14^ARF. [Kato et al., 1998](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0014579398004268), [Zhang et al., 1998](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/9529249/)

**GO evidence.** P42771 has `GO:0090398` *cellular senescence* (IMP, PMID 16243918 and IEA, GO_REF:0000117) and `GO:0090399` *replicative senescence* (IMP, PMID 14720514). It also has `GO:0051726` *regulation of cell cycle* (IDA, PMIDs 15149599 and 8259215, and IMP, PMID 16243918) and `GO:2000045` *regulation of G1/S transition of mitotic cell cycle* (IDA, PMID 10208428, IBA, GO_REF:0000033, and IEA, GO_REF:0000117). Q8N726 has `GO:0090398` (IMP, PMID 14966292), `GO:0051726` (IDA, PMID 9529249 and IEA, GO_REF:0000002) and the narrower `GO:0043517` *positive regulation of DNA damage response, signal transduction by p53 class mediator* (IDA, PMID 9529249, form Q8N726-1). The selected snapshot has no direct CDKN2A entry for the exact term `GO:0006974` or for a telomere-maintenance term. The evidence matrix preserves the two products and the narrower annotation separately. [Phase 4 annotations](go_gene_annotations.csv)

**Independent evidence and limits.** Bond et al. used p16 siRNA. Late-passage normal human fibroblasts temporarily escaped replicative arrest, and small epithelial clusters proliferated in a separate mutant RAS thyroid epithelial model. Chen et al. tested p16 in early-passage human umbilical vein endothelial cells in a study of glycated collagen I. p16 overexpression increased senescence, and RB or p53 depletion blocked the effect of overexpressed p16. These experiments support roles in specific oncogenic and stress contexts. Kato et al. did not perform these experiments, so the separate papers cannot validate the source attribution of CellAge records 153 and 154. Herbig et al. observed p16 upregulation independently of the telomere damage pathway in a subset of human fibroblasts, illustrating why a cell-cycle annotation does not assign p16 to every telomere-driven arrest. [Bond et al., 2004](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/14720514/), [Chen et al., 2006](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16243918/), [Herbig et al., 2004](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/15149599/)

In the p14^ARF study underlying the Q8N726 senescence row, Shamanin and Androphy reported p53-dependent growth arrest and senescence-like morphology after p14^ARF expression in hTERT-immortalized human mammary epithelial cells. The HPV16 E6-immortalized cells resisted the effect. The authors did not detect increased SA-β-gal in their p14^ARF-transduced immortal mammary epithelial cells. Product identity, checkpoint status and readout matter when comparing these experiments. Neither protein experiment measured organismal aging. [Shamanin and Androphy, 2004](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC350550/)

## TERT | The catalytic subunit of telomerase

TERT uses the telomerase RNA template to synthesize telomeric DNA. Bodnar et al. transfected telomerase-negative human RPE-340 retinal pigment epithelial cells and BJ foreskin fibroblasts with hTERT expression vectors. They assayed telomerase activity by TRAP and telomere length by terminal restriction fragments. They followed population doublings and used SA-β-gal staining as a senescence marker. Telomerase-active clones generally maintained or lengthened telomeres, showed less staining and grew past the senescence of negative controls. The authors reported at least 20 additional population doublings. Individual clones varied in activity and telomere length. The gain-of-function experiment supports delay of replicative senescence in these cultures. The study followed growing cells and did not test reversal of established arrest. [Bodnar et al., 1998](https://courses.physics.illinois.edu/phys475/fa2017/PRS/498IBR/Bodnar.Science.1998.Telomeres.pdf), [Beattie et al., 1998](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/9443919/)

**GO evidence.** O14746 has `GO:0007004` *telomere maintenance via telomerase* (IDA, IMP, IBA and NAS) and `GO:0090399` *replicative senescence* (IMP, PMID 9454332). Both terms have an IMP row citing Bodnar et al., the same study as CellAge 845. `GO:0000723` *telomere maintenance* has TAS (PMID 12135483) and IEA (GO_REF:0000002). `GO:2000773` *negative regulation of cellular senescence* has IMP (PMID 11313459) and IDA (PMID 11927518). The related cell-cycle row, `GO:1900087` *positive regulation of G1/S transition of mitotic cell cycle*, has IEA (GO_REF:0000107, with rat UniProtKB:Q673L6 and an Ensembl rat protein). There is no direct TERT row for the exact term `GO:0090398` or for a DNA-damage-labeled term in this selection. [Phase 4 annotations](go_gene_annotations.csv)

The pinned NAS annotation for `GO:0000722` *telomere maintenance via recombination* cites PMID 20351177. Egan and Collins studied human telomerase holoenzyme assembly and subunit interactions. Their paper does not demonstrate a recombination mechanism driven by TERT. The matrix keeps the annotation and flags that gap. [Egan and Collins, 2010](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2876521/)

**Independent evidence and limits.** Minamino et al. reported longer culture lifespan and reduced senescence-associated functional changes after introducing the telomerase catalytic subunit into human aortic endothelial cells. Their finding of SA-β-gal-positive endothelial cells in human coronary lesions came from an observational autopsy series. The culture experiment tests a perturbation and the tissue study documents a marker. Together these findings motivate studies of telomere-limited proliferation. They do not establish sustained tissue benefit, cancer safety or effects on survival in people. [Minamino et al., 2002](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/11927518/)

## Comparison

TP53 restoration and p16 delivery drove arrest in distinct models, whereas TERT expression prolonged proliferation in two cultures that lacked telomerase. p53 and p16 act on checkpoint pathways. TERT addresses telomere attrition as an upstream source of replicative stress. CellAge and GO agree on process involvement at different levels of detail. Both resources cite the same Bodnar study. For TP53 and CDKN2A the cited CellAge papers and selected GO senescence citations provide distinct experiments, with differences in cell type, trigger, gene product and intervention.

The studies use culture-specific measures such as proliferation after removing the intervention, population doublings, morphology and SA-β-gal. None of these measures alone establishes a human aging outcome. The mouse survival result has its own genotype and comparator. López-Otín et al. (2023) discuss how cellular senescence and telomere attrition relate to organismal aging in their review. The framework supplies background and the primary experiments supply the evidence for this report. [López-Otín et al., 2023, review](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36599349/)

## Traceability and interpretation rules

- Start from Phase 4 commit `2df1c64a4d20f7d575790ccff82d79f53a2fae0c`. The [CellAge manifest](../data/raw/cellAge.zip.json) pins archive SHA-256 `943506acb3715a8d507459d69bc9a64c3e7863c809c62457171de03dc0062845`. Filter the original TSV on Entrez IDs 7157, 1029 and 7015 and compare the ordinals to the [Phase 3 shared-gene table](gene_overlap_genes.csv).
- For GO, filter the [Phase 4 annotation table](go_gene_annotations.csv) by these Entrez IDs. Select labels containing `senescen`, `DNA damage`, `telomere`, `cell cycle` or `cyclin-dependent protein serine/threonine kinase`. Include `GO:0008340` separately for the lifespan interpretation. This label filter includes telomere localization and damage checkpoint terms and excludes broader processes such as generic proliferation or stress responses. It selects direct rows without propagating ancestors. The selected rows all have the Phase 4 status `positive`, which means eligible and non-negated. The sign of a biological effect requires the specific term, relation and evidence from the source.
- The matrix contains one row per CellAge ordinal or selected GAF line. The latter is the one-based line in the decompressed GAF, including headers. The [GO manifest](../data/go_provenance.json) pins the GAF and ontology. The matrix retains the original GO identifier, resolved label, exact reference, evidence code, product accession and form, relation, with/from and assigning group. [Phase 4 provenance and attribution](go_biological_processes.md#provenance-and-outputs)
- GO IDA, IMP and IGI indicate direct assay, mutant phenotype and genetic interaction evidence, respectively. IBA indicates phylogenetic inference, ISS indicates sequence or structural similarity, IEA indicates electronic annotation, and TAS and NAS indicate traceable and non-traceable author statements. Human taxon in the GAF identifies the annotation target. The cited model can be non-human. A GO_REF describes an annotation method. [GO evidence code guide](https://geneontology.org/docs/guide-go-evidence-codes/)
- PubMed and Europe PMC supplied the PMIDs, DOIs, titles and publication years for 43 distinct publications in the matrix. For GO citations used to interpret experiments, the matrix records the model, findings and review depth supported by the text examined. A bibliographic verification row identifies a paper without making a claim about its experimental model or findings. Blank fields indicate that a value does not apply or that this review did not establish it. Publication IDs can recur across genes, terms and datasets, so use unique PMIDs when identifying independent papers.
