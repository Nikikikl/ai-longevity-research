# Phase 7B | Evidence integrity audit

The [ledger](phase7b_evidence_ledger.csv) contains **73 records** for TP53, CDKN2A and TERT: 7 CellAge records and 66 selected GO annotations. The 60 rows with publication references contain **43 distinct PMIDs**. The other 13 rows cite GO annotation methods. The earlier review verified bibliographic metadata for the 43 publications; only a subset received full-text or methods/results review. This audit does not extend that depth of review to all publications or to the other 110 shared genes.

## Reuse and experiment provenance

**11 PMIDs recur on 28 rows**. Two recur within CellAge and nine within GO. Bodnar is the only publication cited by both sources; two of its three citations are within GO. Herbig is the only PMID cited for more than one gene in this selection. The counts describe the scoped ledger, not the entire CellAge or GO databases.

| PMID | Record trail | What the reviewed source supports |
| --- | --- | --- |
| [9275177, Sugrue et al.](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC23243/) | TP53; CellAge 870–872 | Wild-type p53 restoration in EJ carcinoma cells produced durable arrest and SA-β-gal staining. Three curation labels do not establish three independently tested triggers. |
| [9607312, Kato et al.](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/9607312/) | CDKN2A; CellAge 152–154 | Recombinant tagged p16 delivered to early-passage fibroblasts induced G1 arrest and features of senescence. The paper does not supply a separate experiment for each CellAge label. |
| [9454332, Bodnar et al.](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.279.5349.349) | TERT; CellAge 845, GAF 27897 and 27918 | hTERT transfection prolonged growth in cultured human retinal pigment epithelial cells and fibroblasts. GO:0007004 and GO:0090399 are distinct assertions citing the same study as CellAge. The study did not test reversal of stable arrest. |
| [15149599, Herbig et al.](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/15149599/) | TP53 GAF 133731 and 133792; CDKN2A GAF 289843 | The abstract separates the ATM–p53–p21 response to telomere damage from p16 upregulation in a subset of cells. A shared citation does not establish a shared mechanism or separate experimental confirmation. |
| [19951988, Binet et al.](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7439003/) | TP53; GAF 133817 and 133818 | The same paper is cited for replicative and oxidative stress-induced premature senescence. Keep both terms. The reviewed replicative knockdown result does not by itself substantiate both claims. |
| [20351177, Egan and Collins](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2876521/) | TERT; GAF 27882 and 27892 | The study characterized telomerase holoenzyme assembly. It does not directly demonstrate the TERT-mediated recombination asserted by GO:0000722 (NAS, line 27882). |

The other repeated PMIDs are 7958916, 9529249, 14744935, 16213212 and 16243918. The ledger retains all their claims and exact locators. No independence conclusion is assigned from the PMID grouping alone.

**Different perturbations can be complementary.** [Bond et al. (PMID 14720514)](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/14720514/) used p16 knockdown and reported temporary escape from arrest in late-passage fibroblasts. This is a distinct reported experiment from Kato's early-passage protein delivery. Their designs are not direct replications and sample independence was not verified. [Shamanin and Androphy (PMID 14966292)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC350550/) examined p14ARF, a different CDKN2A product. They reported arrest in hTERT-immortalized mammary cells but did not detect increased SA-β-gal there.

**Outcomes remain model-specific.** [Ghosh et al. (PMID 29474172)](https://elifesciences.org/articles/32127#fig2) observed increased survival after reducing Trp53 dosage in Sirt6-null mice. GAF line 133834 has a human annotation target; the experiment was in mice. Phase 7A estimates fibroblast expression associations with chronological age and leaves TERT's ineligible estimates blank. Neither result establishes human lifespan extension. See the [case studies](gene_case_studies.md) for interventions, model boundaries and review depths.

## Reproduce and verify

Run from the repository root. The first command checks tracked outputs; the second adds the ignored raw CellAge archive and GAF plus the aggregate JSON generated by the [Phase 7A commands](phase7_data_feasibility.md#provenance-reuse-and-reproduction).

```sh
python3 -B -m scripts.phase7b_ledger --check
python3 -B -m scripts.phase7b_ledger --check --check-raw --metrics data/processed/phase7/metrics.json
```

The generator checks original CellAge ordinals against the shared gene table and matches GO evidence to the Phase 4 CSV. The optional raw check verifies file fingerprints, original CellAge fields and the original GAF line contents. `python3 -B -m scripts.annotate_go --check` independently checks the Phase 4 rebuild. The ledger reuses the existing HAGR and GO snapshots under their [source terms and attribution](../README.md#source-attribution-and-reuse). No articles or raw archives were added. The ledger is a derived index of reviewed results, not a count of independent studies.

## Phase 7D extension

The [extension ledger](phase7d_evidence_ledger.csv) adds eight source rows for FOXO3 and SIRT6. It includes both original CellAge records, two archived GenAge membership rows and four selected GO annotations. The GenAge export carries inclusion categories without PMIDs. The [focused case studies](phase7d_case_studies.md) give the primary studies, review depth, exact source records and comparison with Phase 7A.

Together the two ledgers index 81 rows (9 CellAge, 70 GO and 2 GenAge). The 66 publication-linked rows cite 46 distinct PMIDs. Thirteen PMIDs recur across 33 rows. The Phase 7B ledger retains its original citation counts. Extension counts and Phase 7D PMID lookups use both tables.

- Nagai (PMID 25819580) appears in CellAge 765 and GAF 591460 and 591502. The rows concern senescence, telomere integrity and proliferation in one chondrocyte study. The reviewed methods support short-term phenotypes and leave the `Replicative` subtype unestablished. [Nagai et al.](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25819580/) · [Methods and results summary](https://da.lib.kobe-u.ac.jp/da/kernel/D1006529/D1006529yy.pdf)
- Ghosh (PMID 29474172) links TP53 GAF 133834 to SIRT6 GAF 591513 in the selected rows. This reuse crosses genes and includes common mouse fibroblast comparisons. [Ghosh et al.](https://elifesciences.org/articles/32127)
- TenNapel (PMID 25541994) supports a genotype association with age at death for GAF 591445. This is an observational basis for the lifespan claim. [TenNapel et al.](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0115616)

The expansion separates curated claims from the measurements behind them. Its lookup accepts structured commands for the selected records and reuses the Phase 7A association results. Ten new [development checks](../benchmarks/phase7d_questions.json) matched the expected answers, exact citations and evidence types (10/10 each), including one correctly scoped abstention. The questions and references were prepared from original sources before implementing the lookup. The score checks the command interface against the reviewed data. Phase 7C remains the evaluation of unfamiliar natural-language wording.

```sh
python3 -B -m scripts.phase7d_evidence --check
python3 -B -m scripts.phase7d_evidence --check-raw
python3 -B -m scripts.phase7d_evidence 'record GAF:591445'
python3 -B -m scripts.phase7d_evidence 'pmid 25819580'
python3 -B -m scripts.phase7d_evidence 'association SIRT6'
python3 -B -m scripts.phase7d_evidence --score
```

`--check-raw` additionally verifies both HAGR archive fingerprints, original rows and the selected GAF lines. Lookup and scoring run using tracked files. The [existing attribution](../README.md#source-attribution-and-reuse) applies to the derived records.
